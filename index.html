Data, methodology and attribution

About this atlas

An exploratory geographic reference assembled from the uploaded Noongar language-group polygons, a curated locality list and spelling crosswalk. This is not an official map of Traditional Ownership.

Source permissions. The project maintainer reports permission to redistribute the original language-group shapefiles. The original rightsholder, required attribution and scope of downstream reuse have not yet been independently documented. Treat this first release as provisional until that record is complete.
What the numbers mean

Inside: a coordinate falls within one of the supplied digitised polygons. Near another group: a different group’s mapped edge is within the adjustable distance. Near outer edge: only the containing group’s edge is nearby, possibly a coastline or edge of dataset coverage. On mapped line: within 1 metre of an edge. Multiple matches are shown rather than silently reduced to one group.

Distances: approximate shortest distances to original polygon segments using local latitude-adjusted metre scales. These do not measure cultural uncertainty.

Address and postcode lookups (Geoapify · © OpenStreetMap contributors): When a visitor explicitly selects the online address-search action, the entered street address or postcode is sent to Geoapify. The response is a geocoded reference point, not proof of land or cultural boundaries. Postcode results refer to an area, not a particular property. Searches are not performed on every keystroke. Device location requires explicit browser permission, is not sent to the geocoder, and can be imprecise on desktop computers. Geoapify privacy information ↗

Coordinate quality: the 290 place points include published locality references and estimates reconstructed from the prior workbook specification. Many industrial and natural landmark coordinates have not been independently verified. Consult authoritative geocoding before site-specific use.

National context: The AIATSIS Map of Indigenous Australia is a separate national reference showing general locations of language, social or nation groups. AIATSIS states that its boundaries are not exact or fixed, some information is contested, and the map is not suitable for native title or other land claims. This Atlas does not reproduce or derive its polygons from the AIATSIS map.

Indicative ILUA frames: Six optional frames are constructed from 43 supplied approximate reference vertices for Yued, Whadjuk People, Ballardong People, Gnaala Karla Booja, South West Boojarah, and Wagyl Kaip and Southern Noongar ILUAs. The frames are orientation aids only and are not registered legal boundaries.

Use and maintenance

The Atlas sends address or postcode text to Geoapify only after a visitor explicitly selects the online search action. Basemap providers receive ordinary web tile requests. Device location is requested only when a visitor selects GPS and is not sent to Geoapify by the Atlas.

First release candidate · 17 November 2026. The HTML data is a snapshot; it does not update when an Excel workbook changes. The interface code is newly implemented, inspired by general WBE interaction patterns rather than copied from its GPL-licensed source.

Original GIS polygon source: user-supplied Noongar Language Groups shapefile; the project maintainer confirms redistribution permission, while downstream sublicensing rights are unverified. Basemap tiles retain separate provider terms and attribution.
