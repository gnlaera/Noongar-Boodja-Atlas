Data, methodology and attribution

About this atlas

An exploratory geographic reference assembled from the uploaded Noongar language-group polygons, a curated locality list and spelling crosswalk. This is not an official map of Traditional Ownership.

Source permissions. The project maintainer confirms permission to redistribute the original language-group shapefiles. The rights holder, attribution requirements and any permission to sublicense the geometry for further reuse still need documenting before public publication.
What the numbers mean

Inside: a coordinate falls within one of the supplied digitised polygons. Near another group: a different group’s mapped edge is within the adjustable distance. Near outer edge: only the containing group’s edge is nearby, possibly a coastline or edge of dataset coverage. On mapped line: within 1 metre of an edge. Multiple matches are shown rather than silently reduced to one group.

Distances: approximate shortest distances to original polygon segments using local latitude-adjusted metre scales. These do not measure cultural uncertainty.

Address and postcode lookups (Geoapify · © OpenStreetMap contributors): Submitting a street address or postcode sends that search text to the provider. The response is a geocoded reference point, not proof of land or cultural boundaries. Postcode results refer to an area, not a particular property. Searches are not performed on every keystroke. Device location requires explicit browser permission, is not sent to the geocoder, and can be imprecise on desktop computers. Geoapify privacy information ↗

Coordinate quality: the reference points include published locality references and estimates reconstructed from the prior workbook specification. Many industrial and natural landmark coordinates have not been independently verified. Consult authoritative geocoding before site-specific use.

Places of cultural relevance: This release includes 57 places in the normal Locations system: 36 public-source candidates and 21 records from a supplied Wagyl register. Each selected place shows its source, review status and a limitation statement. Wagyl register records have not yet been verified against a public source or reviewed by the relevant Noongar cultural authority. Points are for orientation, are not heritage-site boundaries, and do not determine cultural authority.

Use and maintenance

Searches of embedded places, groups and coordinates stay in your browser. Text is sent to Geoapify only when you choose the online address or postcode search. Optional third-party tile services receive ordinary web map requests. Device location is requested only when you press the GPS control.

About this map and its sources

This map is a guide only. It shows roughly where Noongar language groups are, the six areas of the South West Native Title Settlement, and some places of cultural relevance.

It does not decide who is a Traditional Owner or who can speak for Country. It is not a source for native title, heritage status or legal boundaries.

Where the information comes from

  • The Settlement and its six agreements: About the Settlement Agreement (South West Aboriginal Land and Sea Council).
  • Agreement (ILUA) boundaries: Native Title (ILUA) (LGATE-067) (Landgate, data.wa.gov.au). For official records, check the National Native Title Tribunal register.
  • Background research: Atlas research notebook (Google).
  • Wider context (further reading): Map of Indigenous Australia (AIATSIS). It shows the general locations of larger groupings across Australia. AIATSIS says it is not exact and not suitable for native title or land claims. It was not used to draw the areas on this map.

Language-group areas come from a supplied source map and are indicative. Place points are approximate. Each place shows its own source when you select it.
