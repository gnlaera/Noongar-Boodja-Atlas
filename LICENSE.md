**Independent alternative permission.** To the extent the Atlas project owns the relevant rights or is authorised to grant the permission, an **Eligible Indigenous Organisation** receives a **royalty-free, non-exclusive, worldwide, continuing licence** to use, copy, publicly display, modify, adapt, combine with other materials, host, publish and redistribute the Atlas-original material for **any lawful activity of that organisation**, including community services, education, heritage, governance, economic development, paid consulting, enterprise activities and revenue-generating work.

**Eligibility** includes:

1. Corporations registered under the *Corporations (Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander) Act 2006* (Cth), including registered native title bodies corporate / prescribed bodies corporate.
2. Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander land councils, Traditional Owner organisations, native title representative bodies, community-controlled charities, health services, educational and cultural organisations, trusts, associations or equivalent bodies genuinely controlled by Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander people.
3. Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander-owned businesses and social enterprises in which Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander people hold majority beneficial ownership **and effective management/control**.
4. Equivalent Indigenous- or First Nations-controlled organisations outside Australia whose governance demonstrably rests with the relevant Indigenous communities.

**The permission includes commercial and revenue-generating use by an Eligible Indigenous Organisation.** This is an intentional exception to the general-public noncommercial restriction, consistent with enabling Indigenous economic self-determination. Eligible organisations need not apply for case-by-case approval to rely on this permission.

An Eligible Indigenous Organisation may engage an external contractor or technical provider to exercise these rights **on its behalf and for its benefit**. That provider acquires no independent right to market, resell, monetise or commercially exploit the Atlas outside that engagement. This permission does **not** permit unrelated commercial entities to acquire unrestricted commercial rights through a nominal intermediary.

Eligible organisations may distribute their adaptations for use under these terms, retain ownership of original enhancements they create, and seek separate terms for their own original work. They cannot grant more rights over Atlas-owned components or third-party components than they possess.

**Conditions:** retain reasonable identification of the underlying Atlas and data providers; retain notices of third-party licences; do not falsely represent a version as officially endorsed, culturally validated or legally authoritative; and comply with applicable privacy, cultural protocols and Aboriginal heritage protections. Attribution may be included in an About screen, documentation or another accessible location appropriate to the medium.

## 4. No general commercial authorisation

Except for the express permission in section 3, no new permission is granted for a non-eligible commercial operator to, for example, sell the Atlas or access to it, commercially license an adapted version, offer it as a monetised feature, place the Atlas behind a paid product, or operate it for a for-profit service beyond what is independently permitted by the relevant public licences or by law. Separate written permission may be requested from the relevant rights holder.

Merely *visiting* a publicly hosted Atlas does not itself indicate that the visitor is granted rights to copy or republish the protected work for commercial purposes. Copyright permissions concern uses controlled by law and cannot prevent viewing of an openly accessible website.

## 6. Cultural information and attribution

The Atlas reproduces **indicative** language-group mapping, not an authoritative determination of Traditional Ownership, cultural boundaries, local group preferences, native title or heritage clearance. Publicly accessible information does not imply that all cultural knowledge is open for reuse. Users should respect the authority and protocols of relevant Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander custodians, including when publishing interpretive work derived from the Atlas.

## 8. Cultural reference records and source audit

A record’s public availability, source quality or inclusion in the Atlas does not grant authority to relicense underlying cultural knowledge or to represent a claim as culturally approved. AI-generated summaries and discovery aids are not treated as source authority. Users must preserve attribution, stated terminology, geographic scope and applicable community protocols.
